## Mark losses and corrections

- **Q2.3 (0/1):** Define the tesla using the force on a current-carrying wire: a 1 m wire carrying 1 A perpendicular to the field experiences a force of 1 N.
- **Q2.4 (1/2):** Convert 0.620 g to 0.620 × 10⁻³ kg before using F = BIL. The flux density is about **0.035 T**.
- **Q3.1 (0/1):** Magnetic force is perpendicular to the proton’s velocity, so it changes direction but does no work and cannot change speed.
- **Q3.2 (0/3):** Combine BQv = mv²/r with t = πr/v, giving t = πm/(BQ), independent of radius.
- **Q4.1 (0/1):** A moderator slows neutrons through collisions; “slowed down” alone does not state the collision mechanism required by the scheme.
- **Q4.3 (1/3):** Credit for fast neutrons released. The answer does not state that the neutron is absorbed to form U-236; “smaller atoms” is not the scheme’s required daughter nuclei/fragments wording.
- **Q4.4 (4/6):** Good coverage of spent fuel, cooling, storage and long-term hazards. Strengthen the treatment stages and pair problems with ways to address them, for example secure deep geological storage and durable containment.
- **Q5.1 (2/3):** Mass defect is approximately 8.83 × 10⁻²⁸ kg and the binding energy is about **497 MeV**. Keep the joule-to-MeV conversion consistent; the working’s 10³ factor is incorrect.
- **Q5.2 (1/2):** Correct energy difference, but the conversion should use 1 MeV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ J. The beta particle’s maximum kinetic energy is **0.475 MeV**.
- **Q5.4 (1/3):** Longest wavelength comes from the smallest transition energy, (2.29 − 2.06) × 10⁻¹³ J. This gives about **8.6 × 10⁻¹² m**.
- **Q6.2 (1/2):** Field lines closer together mean a stronger field. The convergence indicates denser material / greater mass near K.
- **Q6.6 (1/2):** The line should cross the field lines at right angles near N and curve left farther away from N.
- **Q7.4 (2/3):** The surface potential is −7.4 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹, so escape requires 7.4 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹. The initial kinetic energy is 9.8 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹, so escape is possible.
