## Mark losses and corrections

- **Q1.2 (2/3):** The area estimate and conclusion are correct. The scheme's trapezium-rule guidance expects 1 s intervals; the working uses 2 s intervals.
- **Q1.3 (1/2):** Kinetic energy transferred to moving gravel is credited. For the heating mark, name what heats up (gravel, ground or vehicle). Momentum is not an energy store.
- **Q2.3 (1/3):** Diffraction is identified. Explain that graphite spreads the electron waves and that constructive interference gives bright rings; contrast with the spot/patch expected from particles.
- **Q3.1 (1/3):** Reflection is credited. Explain that incoming and reflected waves superpose, producing nodes and antinodes. “In phase” does not explain nodes.
- **Q3.3 (0/1):** First-harmonic wavelength is λ = 2l = 0.66 m. Therefore v = fλ = 370 × 0.66 ≈ **244 m s⁻¹**, not 122 m s⁻¹.
- **Q4.3 (4/5):** The calculation is sound, but the requested precision is 2 significant figures: write **540 Ω** (5.4 × 10² Ω), not 536 Ω.
- **Q4.4 (1/2):** State that the thermistor resistance falls as temperature rises, reducing the parallel combination's resistance and hence the output pd.
- **Q5.1 (1/2):** The scheme allows the speed mark for the sine-based method. The radius should use 5 tan 28.5° + 1 = 3.71 m, giving speed ≈ **4.49 m s⁻¹**.
- **Q5.2 (1/3):** Centripetal force is the horizontal component of tension: T sin 28.5°. The vertical component T cos 28.5° balances weight. T should be about **950 N**.
- **Q5.3 (2/3):** The unbalanced force and pole movement are described. Avoid equating tension with centripetal force; explain the unequal forces on the acrobats.
- **Q6.1 (0/1):** Total kinetic energy is not conserved in an inelastic collision; speed is not the requested quantity.
- **Q6.4 (0/2):** For a perfectly elastic collision, describe the trucks rebounding/moving apart in opposite directions and B moving faster than A.

## Revision list

These include the notes you wrote in the paper (Q5.1, Q8 and Q16) and other topics behind lost marks.

1. **Circular-motion geometry:** resolve an angle measured from the vertical; use tangent for horizontal displacement and establish the orbit radius.
2. **Uncertainty and powers:** percentage uncertainties add for products/quotients; a squared quantity doubles its percentage uncertainty.
3. **Young's double slit:** fringe spacing w = λD/s; know the effect of changing wavelength, screen distance and slit separation.
4. **Diffraction and interference:** explain spreading, constructive interference and bright/dark maxima; distinguish wave patterns from particle impacts.
5. **Stationary waves:** reflected counter-propagating waves superpose; destructive interference creates nodes and constructive interference antinodes.
6. **Harmonics:** for a string fixed at both ends, first-harmonic wavelength is twice its length.
7. **Potential dividers with thermistors:** connect thermistor resistance changes to parallel resistance and output pd.
8. **Conical pendulum forces:** resolve tension into vertical and horizontal components; only the horizontal component supplies centripetal force.
9. **Collisions:** distinguish momentum conservation from kinetic-energy conservation; elastic collisions conserve total kinetic energy.
10. **SHM phase:** acceleration is opposite to displacement, a = −ω²x.
11. **Pendulum energy:** near equilibrium, height gain and gravitational potential energy vary approximately as displacement squared.
12. **Calculation presentation:** follow the requested significant figures and state the physical comparison supporting a conclusion.
