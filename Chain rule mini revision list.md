# Chain rule mini revision list

A short practice list to strengthen chain rule fluency before adding it to a wider weaknesses mock.

## Practise in this order

1. **Spot the layers:** For each expression, name the outer function and inner expression before differentiating. Include powers, roots, reciprocals, and trigonometric functions.
2. **Power of a function:** Differentiate forms such as \((2x+5)^4\) and \((x^2-3x+1)^3\). Practise keeping the inside unchanged while differentiating the outside, then multiplying by the inside derivative.
3. **Trigonometric functions with an inside:** Differentiate \(\sin(3x)\), \(\cos(2x-1)\), and \(\sin(x^2)\). Check that the inside derivative is included and the angle stays intact.
4. **Powers of trigonometric functions:** Practise expressions such as \(\sin^2(3x)\) and \(\cos^3(2x)\). Identify all the layers: power, trig function, then angle.
5. **Exponentials:** Differentiate \(e^{2x+1}\), \(e^{x^2}\), and \(e^{3x^2-x}\). Focus on multiplying by the derivative of the exponent; don’t multiply by the exponent itself.
6. **Logarithms:** Differentiate \(\ln(2x+1)\), \(\ln(x^2+4)\), and \(\ln((x+1)^2)\). Practise the reciprocal of the inside multiplied by the inside derivative.
7. **Mixed, multi-layer questions:** Work through expressions such as \((\ln(1+x^2))^3\) and \(e^{\sin(2x)}\), writing one factor for each chain-rule layer.
8. **Explain your working:** For a selection of answers, label each factor with what it came from. For example: “outer derivative” and “inside derivative.”
9. **Check by substitution:** After differentiating, substitute a simple value such as \(x=0\) into both your working and simplified answer to see whether they agree.

## Key checks

- Did I differentiate every nested layer?
- Did I preserve the original inside expression in the outside derivative?
- For \(\sin(kx)\) and \(\cos(kx)\), did I include the factor \(k\)?
- For \(e^{u}\), did I keep \(e^u\) and multiply by \(du/dx\)?
- For \(\ln(u)\), did I use \((1/u)(du/dx)\)?
- Did I avoid confusing \(d/dx\), the instruction, with \(dy/dx\), the derivative of \(y\)?
