Section B: Statistics

Sampling, regression and correlation

B.1. A college has 240 Year 12 students and 160 Year 13 students. Describe how to select a stratified random sample of 40, including sampling frames and the number chosen from each year. [4]

B.2. A regression line predicts exam score from revision hours and has gradient \(5.60\) marks per hour. Interpret the gradient and estimate the change for an additional \(0.5\) hours. [2]

An unlabelled horizontal scale runs \(0,2,4,\ldots,24\) and represents one of the weather variables; identify a plausible large-data-set variable and explain what clue you would use. [3]

Histograms and grouped data

B.4. A histogram has class \(0\text{--}10\) with frequency 12 and bar width \(2\text{ cm}\). A second class \(10\text{--}20\) is \(3\text{ cm}\) wide. Find its frequency. The total frequency is \(80\); find the percentage in the second class. [4]

B.5. Grouped data have classes \(0\text{--}10\), \(10\text{--}20\), \(20\text{--}30\) with frequencies 5, 9, 6. Estimate the mean and standard deviation using midpoints. State the within-class assumption. If one raw value changes from \(2.3\) to \(3.2\), explain whether the grouped estimate changes. [5]

Probability and binomial models

B.9. A fair coin is tossed twice. Define events \(A=\text{first toss is heads}\) and \(B=\text{exactly one head}\). Determine whether A and B are independent, showing the relevant probabilities. [3]

Section C: Mechanics

Vectors, integration and projectiles

C.1. A particle has velocity \(\mathbf v(t)=6t\mathbf i-4t^2\mathbf j\) \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) and initial position \(\mathbf r(0)=-20\mathbf i+20\mathbf j\) m. Find \(\mathbf r(t)\), then its position at \(t=2\). Distinguish position from displacement in your working. [5]

C.2. A projectile lands with velocity components \(10\sqrt3\) \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) horizontally and \(-9.6\) \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) vertically. Find its speed and direction, stating clearly whether it is above or below the horizontal. [3]

C.3. Two projectiles meet after 2 s. At that instant P is at height \(0.4\,\mathrm m\); Q was launched from ground with horizontal component \(25-10\sqrt3\) \(\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\). Given equal horizontal positions at impact, find Q’s launch speed and angle. Use separate horizontal and vertical equations and show the component arithmetic. [6]

Equilibrium, friction and moments

C.4. A rests on a rough plane at angle \(\alpha\), where \(\sin\alpha=\frac5{13}\) and \(\cos\alpha=\frac{12}{13}\). Its mass is \(2m\) and \(\mu=\frac23\). Compare the downslope component of weight with maximum friction. State the condition for equilibrium and conclude whether A can remain at rest. [5]

C.5. A uniform ramp is supported at A and C. Its weight W acts at a point distance d from A along a ramp of length L. Write the moment equation about A and find the reaction at C. Explain how that reaction changes if the centre of mass shifts toward A. [4]

C.6. A particle moves in a horizontal circle on a string of length \(5\,\mathrm m\) at \(28.5^\circ\) to the vertical. A pole of radius \(1\,\mathrm m\) is at the circle’s centre. Find the orbit radius, speed, tension for a \(95\,\mathrm{kg}\) acrobat, and identify the horizontal and vertical components of tension. [6]

Modelling and interpretation

C.8. A projectile’s speed is \(12.6\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) at an angle \(52.5^\circ\) above horizontal. Find its velocity components after \(0.8\,\mathrm s\) under gravity \(9.8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\). Explain how a small error in a component can affect a later speed or angle, and why \(g=9.8\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}\) is a model approximation. [3]
